# Logic Requirements

This directory contains research and documentation relating to the requirements and conditions that govern access to locations, world traversal, events, and progression in the randomizer.

## Scope

Requirement research may include:

- Item requirements.
- Ability requirements.
- Event requirements.
- Age requirements.
- Equipment requirements.
- Environmental conditions.
- Dungeon prerequisites.
- Conditional access.
- Multiple possible methods of access.
- Optional tricks.
- Requirement dependencies.

## Requirement Documentation

Where possible, requirements should be documented in a way that distinguishes:

- Mandatory conditions.
- Alternative conditions.
- Optional techniques.
- Context-dependent conditions.
- Assumptions.
- Unverified requirements.

Research should explain the reasoning behind a requirement rather than merely recording a conclusion.

## Requirement Relationships

Requirements may involve combinations of items, events, abilities, or other conditions.

Research should document how these conditions interact, including where multiple independent methods of satisfying a requirement exist.

## Relationship to Other Domains

- **Locations:** Requirements determine whether individual checks are accessible.
- **World:** Requirements may govern transitions between regions.
- **Items:** Requirements use item properties and capabilities.
- **Settings:** Configuration may enable, disable, or alter requirements.
- **Testing:** Requirements should be tested against documented scenarios.

## Research Guidelines

- Record the conditions under which a requirement applies.
- Distinguish observed behavior from interpretation.
- Document alternate methods when known.
- Record limitations and unresolved questions.
- Avoid prematurely formalizing requirements into a specific programming language or data format.

## Future Development

Requirement research may eventually be translated into structured logic rules used by a randomizer engine, accessibility solver, or tracker.

Formal representations should be established after the requirements have been sufficiently researched and documented.
