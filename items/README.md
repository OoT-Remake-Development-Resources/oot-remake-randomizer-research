# Items

This directory contains research and documentation relating to the items available in the Ocarina of Time remake and their potential roles in a randomizer.

Item research aims to establish a consistent understanding of item identities, classifications, properties, capabilities, and effects.

## Scope

Item research may include:

- Item identification.
- Item names and descriptions.
- Item categories.
- Item variants.
- Item capabilities.
- Item effects.
- Progressive items.
- Consumable items.
- Equipment.
- Quest items.
- Item acquisition and behavior.
- Original item placement and rewards.

## Directory Structure

- **[catalog/](catalog/README.md)** – Research and documentation relating to individual item identities.
- **[categories/](categories/README.md)** – Classification and grouping of items.
- **[effects/](effects/README.md)** – Item functionality, effects, and capabilities.

Additional categories may be established as research develops.

## Item Principles

Item identities and basic metadata should be documented separately from the logic rules that determine what an item permits.

Where possible, research should distinguish between an item's observable behavior, its potential logical capabilities, and assumptions made for randomizer implementation.

## Relationship to Other Domains

- **Logic:** Items may provide capabilities or satisfy requirements.
- **Locations:** Items may be associated with original rewards or randomized placement.
- **Trackers:** Item identities and properties may be used to represent inventory and progression.
- **Settings:** Configuration may affect item pools, item behavior, or starting inventory.
- **Testing:** Item behavior and interactions may require verification.

## Research Guidelines

- Use consistent item naming.
- Document relevant item variants.
- Distinguish observed effects from logical interpretations.
- Record references and supporting evidence.
- Document unresolved questions and discrepancies.
- Avoid duplicating item identities across other domains.

## Future Development

Item research may eventually support structured item catalogs, logic systems, tracker applications, and randomizer generation.

Formal data structures and schemas should be established after sufficient research has identified the requirements.
