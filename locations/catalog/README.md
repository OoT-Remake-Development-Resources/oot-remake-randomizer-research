# Location Catalog

This directory contains research and documentation relating to the master catalog of individual item-bearing locations in the Ocarina of Time remake.

The catalog aims to establish a consistent record of every known check, including its identity, name, area, characteristics, and relevant supporting information.

## Scope

The catalog may include:

- Individual item-bearing locations.
- Unique identifiers and naming conventions.
- Human-readable location names.
- Area associations.
- Check types.
- Original rewards, when known.
- Relevant location attributes.
- Verification status.
- References and supporting evidence.

## Catalog Principles

### Consistent Identity

Each individual location should eventually have a unique and stable identity that distinguishes it from other checks.

Identifiers should not rely exclusively on display names, which may change or have alternate naming conventions.

### Avoid Duplication

The catalog should serve as the primary source of location identity and basic metadata.

Logic requirements, tracker display configuration, and other specialized data should reference catalog entries rather than unnecessarily duplicating their definitions.

### Document Uncertainty

Unconfirmed information should not be presented as established fact.

Where information is uncertain or disputed, document the uncertainty and relevant evidence.

## Potential Location Information

The following are potential catalog attributes to investigate. This is not a finalized schema.

- Identifier
- Name
- Description
- Area
- Check type
- Original reward
- Conditions
- References
- Verification status
- Notes

## Research and Verification

Catalog entries should be supported by observations, documentation, or other evidence when available.

Research should distinguish between:

- Directly observed information.
- Verified findings.
- Information derived from external sources.
- Assumptions and hypotheses.
- Unresolved discrepancies.

## Future Development

The catalog may eventually be represented as structured data and used by randomizer logic, tracker applications, mapping tools, and other projects.

Formal schema definitions and data formats should be developed separately after the catalog's requirements become clearer.
